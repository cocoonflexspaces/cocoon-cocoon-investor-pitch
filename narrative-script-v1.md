## OPENING (before the deck, in the room)

> "Before I start — I want you to think about the last extraordinary private space you walked into in New York. A penthouse, a loft, a townhouse that felt like a gallery. Someone owns that space. It sits empty most of the year. And there's a version of that story where that owner is generating serious income from it — and where a brand's next product launch, activation, or executive retreat happens there instead of a hotel ballroom. That's what Cocoon does. Let me show you how."

## SLIDE 1 — Cover

**On screen:** Logo + "Unconventional Spaces. Extraordinary Experiences." + one full-bleed Casa image.

**Say:**
> "Cocoon is a managed private space marketplace. We give extraordinary, design-forward homes and residences a second life as venues for productions, brand activations, and events — and we do it in a way that works for everyone: the property owner, the client booking it, and us."

## SLIDE 2 — The Moment

**On screen:** Three converging forces. Clean visual — not bullets.
- NYC regulatory shift (short-term rentals)
- Proposed NYC wealth asset tax
- The IRL brand activation boom

**Say:**
> "Three things are converging right now that make this the right moment.
>
> First, NYC regulation. Short-term vacation rental laws have made it much harder for high-value property owners to monetize through platforms like Airbnb. Owners with extraordinary spaces are actively looking for other options. We are that option.
>
> Second — and this is the more powerful one — the proposed millionaire asset tax being considered in New York. Wealthy families and family offices with Manhattan properties they use two weeks a year are going to be heavily incentivized to make those assets work. We already onboarded our first family office property at the end of 2025 — a stunning gallery-type space in Williamsburg. That's the beginning of a meaningful pipeline. When that tax passes, our phone rings.
>
> Third, the demand side: In Real Life is having its moment. Brands are pulling budget away from digital and investing it in physical experiences. Brand activations, immersive showrooms, exclusive client events — this is one of the fastest-growing categories in experiential marketing, and it's global. LA, Miami, Paris, London, Tokyo. The bookings are getting bigger. We'll come back to exactly how much bigger.
>
> Cocoon sits at the intersection of all three."

---

## SLIDE 3 — The Problem

**On screen:** Two panels. Left: property owner. Right: the brands and production companies who need spaces.

**Say:**
> "On one side, you have property owners. Extraordinary homes and spaces — the kind of places brands would pay serious money to be in. But these owners are not in the events business. They don't have insurance frameworks, they don't want to manage bookings or host strangers, and they're not going to take on liability without serious assurances. So the best spaces in New York stay locked.
>
> On the other side, you have the demand. Production companies, brands running activations, luxury clients who need something that a hotel ballroom or a commercial studio simply cannot deliver. They want unconventional, design-forward spaces. They want someone accountable. They want it to work perfectly — because when it doesn't, their brand pays the price.
>
> Peerspace and Giggster are connector marketplaces. They list the space and step aside. The property owner handles everything — communication, hosting, insurance, on-site issues. That works for a $5,000 offsite booking.
>
> But it doesn't work for this: we are on the verge of closing a booking with Fendi — a seven-day exclusive showroom for their top 2% clientele. The space alone, plus our concierge services, is tracking toward $500,000. Fendi has their brand reputation, their most important clients, and their entire activation budget on the line. They are not going to book that from a homeowner with no insurance, no liability structure, and no one answerable when something goes wrong on day four.
>
> This isn't the only booking like this. Last year we closed a $250,000 activation for Delta and American Express. A $160,000 booking for Djerf Avenue. In 2024, nearly $100,000 for Lego. The bookings are getting larger every year.
>
> The bigger the booking, the more Cocoon becomes the only option."

## SLIDE 4 — What Cocoon Is

**On screen:** Clean two-sided model diagram. Property owners on one side, clients on the other. Cocoon in the middle with five pillars: Trust Protocols · Dedicated Casa Managers · Curated Marketing · Concierge Services · Full Asset Management.

**Say:**
> "Cocoon is not a listing platform. We are a fully managed marketplace. That distinction is everything.
>
> For property owners — we take over. We handle photography and listings, marketing campaigns specific to their space, all booking communications, on-site management during every single event, cleaning, maintenance, and we pay them. They approve bookings and get paid. That's it. Nothing else touches them.
>
> That level of service is why we can unlock spaces that no platform can. The owner of Casa Olympia — a high-profile athlete — told us directly he would never have considered renting his home without our protocols. We've presented to co-op boards. We've onboarded family office properties. These are not Peerspace spaces.
>
> For clients — they get a curated portfolio of spaces that feel genuinely unconventional and design-forward. Every space is professionally managed. Every booking has a Cocoon Casa Manager on-site. And if they need catering, photography, production support, floral — that's our Super Concierge. One call."

## SLIDE 7 — Business Model Today

**On screen:** Revenue breakdown by category. Commission structure. Gross margin visual.

**Say:**
> "Revenue comes from four sources today: space rental commissions, Casa Manager fees, cleaning services, and our Super Concierge — catering, photography, production, hospitality bundled into a single service layer.
>
> The gross margin profile is what sets this apart from a typical marketplace. Base bookings run at 45–53% gross margin. When Super Concierge is attached — which is increasingly the case on larger bookings — margins go up to 70%. As we expand the SC offering, we expect our average gross margin to settle in the 60–65% range. That's a strong, defensible margin for a business of this type.
>
> Two emerging plays worth flagging. First: we've been accepting select bookings at a discount in exchange for social media content collaborations with brands. Our spaces are visually extraordinary — brands want to be seen in them. We're expanding this deliberately as a near-zero-cost acquisition channel. Second: we've had inbound interest from real estate investors — typically earlier-stage buyers — asking us to consult on which NYC properties to acquire specifically to monetize through Cocoon. We have seven years of operational data on what makes a space perform. That's a consulting offering we'll formalize mid-term."

## SLIDE 8 — The Platform Opportunity

**On screen:** Evolution diagram. Three phases: Managed Services → Own Marketplace → Platform with Recurring Revenue. Each phase labeled with what it unlocked.

**Say:**
> "This is the slide that matters most for this conversation.
>
> We started as a managed services business. Commission on bookings, service fees. That model works and it's profitable.
>
> Then we built our own marketplace — Zoe's platform is live, our bookings are flowing through it, we're no longer paying 30% to Peerspace for leads. That was phase two.
>
> Phase three is where the investment thesis gets interesting. We're transitioning from a high-margin services business into a platform with recurring revenue on both sides of the marketplace.
>
> On the supply side: we cap each city portfolio at 200 properties. That scarcity is intentional — it protects quality, and it creates real platform value. We then build subscription-based relationships with those owners: monthly access fees, booking boost placements, design consulting to improve their property's performance. Property owners become paying participants in the platform, not just passive recipients of our management services.
>
> On the demand side: our Discovery Portfolio and Super Concierge marketplace flip to a membership or access-fee model. Production companies and event agencies pay for privileged access to the invite-only portfolio. Vendors pay for boosted placement inside the Super Concierge. And eventually, clients who haven't booked a Cocoon property can pay for access to the SC network.
>
> Four revenue streams from one platform. Three of them recurring. The foundation — the marketplace, the protocols, the brand — is already built."
